Sidekick

Privacy

Effective 2 October 2026 (updated for 2.6.2)

The short version Sidekick has no account, no servers, no ads and no analytics. Your files go straight between your own devices, encrypted. Nothing is sent to us.

What Sidekick keeps on your device

  • Your settings: the device name, theme, sound, the folder for received files, and what paired devices may do.
  • Your paired devices: each one's name, device ID, certificate fingerprint, and the keys that let the two of you talk securely. These are kept in the system's protected storage: the Keychain on iPhone, storage encrypted with the Android Keystore on Android, Windows' built-in data protection on Windows, and a file only your user account can read on the Mac.
  • Its own security identity: a certificate and private key made on the device when Sidekick first starts.
  • Files you receive, in the folder you choose. On iPhone and Android, received photos and videos go to Photos or the gallery instead (on Android, in Pictures/Sidekick and Movies/Sidekick).

None of this is uploaded anywhere. Removing Sidekick removes it, except the files you received, which stay in their folder.

What Sidekick shares, and with whom

Nearby devices

So your devices can find each other, Sidekick announces itself on your local network and over Bluetooth: its device name, a random device ID, the kind of device (for example Mac or Android), its Sidekick version, its network port, the fingerprint of its certificate, which features it offers, and (over Bluetooth) whether it's on Wi-Fi. Anyone on the same network or within Bluetooth range can see this announcement. It contains no files and no other personal content.

On iPhone and Mac, Sidekick also announces its random device ID over Apple's peer-to-peer Wi-Fi (the link AirDrop uses), so two Apple devices can reach each other with no shared network. Apple devices within Wi-Fi range can see that ID; anything sent over the link is encrypted, like on your network.

On Windows and Mac, closing Sidekick's window keeps it running in the tray (the menu bar on a Mac), so your devices can still reach it; choose Quit there to stop it. On Android, a small "Ready to receive" notification shows while it runs in the background.

Paired devices

A device can only do anything with yours after you pair them with a 6-digit code or a QR code. Paired devices can then:

  • ask to send you files. Sidekick asks you to Accept first, unless you turn off “Ask before receiving files” in Settings;
  • browse your files and use your mouse and keyboard. Both are allowed by default; turn either off in Settings under “What paired devices can do here”. Using the mouse and keyboard also needs the system's Accessibility permission on the Mac and Android, which you grant yourself.
  • share your clipboard: text you copy goes to your paired devices nearby, and what they copy lands in yours (turn it off in Settings under “Share clipboard”). Every device sends what you copy by itself; Android and iPhone only while Sidekick is open (they let no app read the clipboard in the background), and an iPhone asks “Allow Paste?” unless you allow it in Settings. A copy is delivered to a device that's out of reach when it's back, for a few minutes. What password managers mark as secret is never sent, and Sidekick keeps no copy;
  • ping your device: it rings until you tap Found It! on it (on Android at alarm volume, so you hear it on silent).

You can unpair a device at any time.

Encryption

Everything between paired devices is encrypted: over Wi-Fi with TLS, checked against each device's own certificate; over Bluetooth with AES-GCM keys agreed when you paired (SPAKE2, so the code itself never travels).

Permissions, and why

Local network
All devices: to find and talk to your devices.
Bluetooth
All devices: to connect when one of them isn't on Wi-Fi. While both are, Sidekick only uses Wi-Fi.
Notifications
iPhone and Android: to show a file request while Sidekick is in the background, with Accept and Decline, and on Android its progress and the "Ready to receive" notice.
Camera
iPhone and Android: only to scan a pairing QR code. The picture is read on the device and is never saved or sent.
Photos
iPhone: only the photos you pick to send, and adding the photos and videos you receive to your library. Adding doesn't let Sidekick see the rest of your library.
Files and “All files access”
Android: to save files you receive and, if you allow it, let paired devices browse your files.
Location
Android 12 and older only: those versions require it for Bluetooth and Wi-Fi scanning. Sidekick never reads or stores your location.
Accessibility
Mac and Android: only so a paired device can move the pointer and type, when you allow it.
Keep Wi-Fi awake
Android: while Sidekick runs in the background, so your devices can still reach the phone with its screen off. It asks for nothing and reads nothing.
Direct Wi-Fi
When two devices share no Wi-Fi, one opens a small private Wi-Fi network (an Android phone's hotspot, or a Windows PC's Wi-Fi Direct network) and the other joins it (Android and iPhone ask you first where the system requires it). The network's name and password go only to the other device: over encrypted Bluetooth, or in the QR code shown on screen. It shares no mobile data, and closes when you're done.
Firewall
Windows: the installer lets Sidekick accept connections from devices on private networks, and from the local subnet on public networks (for the direct link). Uninstalling removes these rules.

What we don't do

  • No account or sign-in.
  • No analytics, tracking, advertising or crash reports.
  • No servers: Sidekick never contacts us, and it doesn't go online to check for updates.
  • We don't sell or share data. We don't have any.

This website and the downloads

The download page uses no cookies and no analytics. It remembers your light or dark choice and color in your browser (local storage). It loads its fonts from Google Fonts, so your browser connects to Google's servers (see Google's privacy policy); this page doesn't. The downloads are served by GitHub (see GitHub's privacy statement). If you install Sidekick through another service, such as AltStore, SideStore, Sideloadly or the Microsoft Store, that service's own privacy policy applies to it.

Children

Sidekick doesn't collect personal data from anyone, including children.

Changes
